# Harness Manager launch copy

## Positioning

The free, open-source Mac workspace for your AI stack.

For developers using several AI coding tools, Harness Manager brings discovery, installed tools, updates, configuration, model rankings, and ecosystem news into one native workspace.

## Headline

Your AI stack. Under control.

## Product Hunt tagline

Manage your AI stack and compare models, right on your Mac

## Short description

Your AI tools keep multiplying. Give them one home. Discover harnesses, MCPs, and skills, review updates, see what’s running, and compare models across 31 ranking collections. Harness Manager is a native Mac app, free and open source.

## Launch post

I built Harness Manager to keep up with an AI setup that kept growing.

It brings the tools and decisions into one Mac app: what’s installed, what needs an update, what’s running, and what to try next.

You can browse harnesses, MCPs, and skills; inspect provider and tool configuration; and read sourced news. The new Benchmarks page adds model comparisons and 31 ranking collections, from coding and design to reasoning and local models, with data from Modelgrep.

It’s free and open source. Download the app, drag it to Applications, and open your workspace.

This is an early preview for macOS 14 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. The current build is not yet notarized, so macOS may require approval in Privacy & Security on first launch.

## Message discipline

- Lead with managing an AI stack and getting back to work. Explain “harness” through familiar tools such as Claude Code and Codex.
- Show the actual app. The seventh gallery slide introduces model rankings; the website offers Coding, Design, and Intelligence previews.
- Say “compare published model rankings,” not “run benchmarks.” Harness Manager retrieves results; it does not evaluate models itself.
- Credit Modelgrep. Each collection has its own scoring context; the ranking screenshots show data at capture time.
- Keep the download as the main action. Source access is a benefit, not a requirement to build the app yourself.
- Avoid unverified adoption numbers, “best” claims, or promises of automatic app updates that the release does not support.
